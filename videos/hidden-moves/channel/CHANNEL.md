## Episodes: upload copy
### EP.01 · Why gas stations come in pairs
- **Title:** Why rival gas stations always end up next to each other ⛽
- **Description:** It looks like bad business, but it's a game. Pip and Dot keep sliding toward the middle until neither can move. Hotelling's Law (Harold Hotelling, "Stability in Competition", 1929), shown with the simple road model. Real towns add other reasons too, like busy corners.
- **Hashtags:** #gametheory #economics #hotellingslaw #animation #didyouknow #shorts
- **Pinned comment:** Where have you seen two rivals side by side? Wrong answers only. 👇

### EP.02 · The winner actually lost
- **Title:** Why the auction winner usually overpaid 🔨
- **Description:** When the true value is uncertain, the highest bidder is usually the one who overestimated most. It's called the winner's curse. Bazerman & Samuelson's 1983 jar-of-coins experiment found that winners lost money on average, and the effect was first noticed in auctions for oil drilling rights. On-screen numbers are an illustrative example.
- **Hashtags:** #winnerscurse #gametheory #auctions #psychology #animation #shorts
- **Pinned comment:** Ever won something and instantly regretted it? 👇

### EP.03 · Why everyone stands at concerts
- **Title:** Why everyone ends up standing at concerts 🎤
- **Description:** One person stands to see better, the person behind them has to stand too, and soon everyone is standing with the same view and sorer feet. Each move makes sense alone, but together they cancel out. Economists call it an arms race.
- **Hashtags:** #gametheory #armsrace #concerts #psychology #animation #shorts
- **Pinned comment:** Stand or stay seated? Defend your answer. 👇

### EP.04 · The option nobody buys
- **Title:** Why companies sell an option nobody buys 🦆
- **Description:** The Economist once offered web-only for $59, print-only for $125, and print + web for $125. In Dan Ariely's classroom test (Predictably Irrational), 84% chose print + web. Remove the useless option and that fell to 32%. Meet the decoy.
- **Hashtags:** #decoyeffect #pricing #behaviouraleconomics #marketing #animation #shorts
- **Pinned comment:** Spotted a decoy in the wild? Drop it below. 👇

## Launch checklist
1. Claim the same handle on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram.
2. Upload `profile-pip.png` everywhere, then add the banner and watermark on YouTube.
3. Paste the bios. Pin a "Start here" playlist on YouTube and create the Instagram highlights from the four `ig-highlight` covers.
4. Post EP.01 first (strongest hook), then EP.04, EP.02 and EP.03, about 2–3 days apart so each one gets a fair read in analytics.
5. For each post, upload the matching `cover-epXX.jpg` as the cover, paste the title, description and hashtags, then pin the comment.
6. After 48 hours, check retention at 1 second and 3 seconds, average view duration, shares per view and follows per view before choosing the next topic.
